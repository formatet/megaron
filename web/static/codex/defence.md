In a game where nobody is online all the time, defence is mostly a matter of **knowing early** — and then having time to answer.

## You are told when something comes

When a foreign army is seen marching, you get a *foreign march sighted* notification — often with where it is heading and roughly when it lands. It only fires if one of your eyes sees it: a settlement, a unit, a messenger on the road ([[sight]]).

## Why the defender has time

Orders are physical ([[runners]]). The attacker's army walks the whole distance to you; your answer usually has a much shorter way to go — from your city to the nearby units, or to the garrison already there. **Distance is the defender's ally.**

## Buying warning time

- Post units in **sentry** on the approaches — roads, river crossings, the coast ([[stances]]).
- Keep a ship at sea near your coast.
- A well-filled [[sitos|granary]] buys time against a [[sieges|siege]].
- Walls absorb damage in [[battle]] ([[buildings]]).

When you come back after being away, **foreign marches are among the first things to read** ([[coming-back]]).

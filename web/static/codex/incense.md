**Incense is the smoke the gods like best, and it grows in very few places.** It is a rare religious good: no building makes it, no recipe needs it, and nothing in your city fails without it. What it does is make an offering count for more.

## Where it comes from

Incense is found only on marked hexes of dry scrub (maquis) and semi-desert — the ground that gives almost nothing else. A marked hex shows a small amber drop on the map and the word **Incense** in its inspect panel. A deposit in your [[catchment]] needs no building: **two citizens** placed on the hex ([[citizens]]) gather it. There are about ten such places in a world of the usual size, scattered over both halves of the map and more of them in the east. Most Wanaxes will have none, which is why it is carried and traded ([[trade]]).

## What it does at the altar

Any good can be offered, and wine and oil are offerings too ([[rites]]) — wine is also food, so a hungry city should keep it. Incense is different in one way: **a gift made mostly of incense can please the gods further than any other gift**. Grain and bronze, however much you pile on the altar, reach a limit; an offering of incense reaches past it. It never replaces your standing with the gods ([[kharis]]) — a god who does not hear you is not bought — but it is the best use of what you carry. Each god's taste is shown in the Offer row and in `keryx rite --list`; the gods of sight and omens love it most.

## Some history

Bronze Age temples burned resin, not one perfume. Around the east of the Mediterranean the common kind was the sap of the terebinth tree (*Pistacia*), gathered on the dry hills of Kna'an and Cyprus and shipped in jars. The wreck at Uluburun, sunk off the south coast of Anatolia at the end of the thirteenth century before our era, carried about a hundred and fifty such jars among its copper and tin. Amphorae from Kna'an turn up in Egypt with traces of the same resin. The rarer frankincense and myrrh came to Egypt from Punt, far down the Red Sea, in small and precious loads; labdanum, scraped from the rock-rose shrubs of Crete, was the Minoan smell. Here they are all one good, **Incense**.

Everything your cities make, eat and trade is a **good**. The **Economy (⚖)** drawer shows your goods across every settlement, in four tabs: **Goods**, **Transfer**, **Automation** and **Wants**.

| Good | What it is for |
|---|---|
| **Grain, fish, livestock** | Food, eaten in that order ([[food]]). |
| **Timber, stone** | The material of nearly every [[buildings|building]]. Early on, timber is almost always your bottleneck. |
| **Cedar** | Found only where there is a deposit, but a cedar forest yields far more wood than an olive grove. Levels your best buildings; builds war galleys. **Cedar stands in for timber:** whatever a cost needs in timber and you lack, your cedar covers one for one. |
| **Copper, tin** | The two halves of bronze — never found together ([[bronze]]). |
| **Bronze** | Elite infantry, war chariots, war galleys, the best walls. |
| **Wine, oil** | Offerings for [[rites|prayers]], food variety, good trade goods. |
| **Silver** | The currency, and the only thing that pays soldiers ([[silver]]). |

## The four tabs

- **Goods** — stock, production and use of each good, city by city. The settlements table compares people, grain made minus grain eaten, food days and shortage status; select a city to close Economy and open its City drawer. Goods piling up with nothing using them are marked — see [[surplus]].
- **Transfer** — move goods between your own cities ([[transfers]]).
- **Automation** — [[routes|standing orders]] that move goods for you while you are away.
- **Wants** — shortages and surpluses in every city you have [[contact|contacted]]: where to [[trade]].

## Offerings and trade

The gods weigh goods differently: bronze can count for far more than grain. **Kult**, at the temple, and `keryx rite --list` show their current reckoning before each god's preferences. What the world can spare and what the god favours both matter ([[rites]]). Wanaxes agree their own trade prices.

Production per day is output before meals and other uses. Food net combines grain and fish minus population demand. The city has one stock of each good; food coverage counts its grain and fish together. Shortfall waiting estimates assume current production continues, before other use.

Food for people combines grain and fish minus the population’s meal. **Food after army** subtracts the grain paid by this city to its supported troops as well. The army eats grain first, not fish; its silver pay is shown separately. Population growth still uses the population-only food balance.

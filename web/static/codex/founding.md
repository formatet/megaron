Founding your first city — the **metropolis** — is the one decision in Megaron you cannot undo. The Host dissolves forever.

## Read the ground first

The [[horde|Host]] panel shows the terrain and four short assessments for the hex you stand on: **Feeds itself: yes/no**, **Timber: yes/no**, **Stone: yes/no**, and **Ships: yes / never — inland**. An inland metropolis can never build a harbour or ships ([[buildings]]). They use the existing forecast for known ground; unknown means the forecast is unavailable. The [[catchment]] you would claim glows on the map. Open **Details** for the full founding forecast, deposits, production, gifts, each catchment hex, and the Host’s people and supplies. It updates as you walk.

Under **Details**, the forecast gives the grain your fields would produce against what your people would eat, and one of three verdicts:

- **It feeds itself** — the fields cover the mouths with room to spare.
- **Marginal** — positive but thin. Draft one cohort and you are eating into the food stock.
- **It does not feed itself** — you are told how many game days your carried grain buys, and whether a Farm would fix it. If it says a farm will not help, there is no farmable ground in reach at all.

The gap between *marginal* and *does not feed itself* has killed more young cities than any war. See [[food]].

In **Details**, below the grain line, look for **deposits**: copper, tin, silver, cedar. A site with metal under it is worth walking extra game days for. A site with none is not a mistake — you can buy metal ([[trade]]) — but you will be buying it from someone who walked those game days. Read [[bronze]] before you decide.

## Where you cannot found

Not on sea, mountain, river or semi-desert, and **not within two hexes of another settlement** — city centres stand at least **three** hexes apart. Closer than that, the game refuses and tells you how far to move. At three or more your [[catchment]] may share ground with a neighbour's; who works a shared hex is told in [[catchment]].

## Settling

The Host panel follows its current physical position, including on the march. **Waiting** explains why the current ground cannot be settled; **Can found here** means the site is valid. If the Host has moved while you were confirming, reopen the panel before trying again.

Press **⚒ Found the metropolis here** at the bottom of the Host panel. Read the warning beside the button, then press **Found the metropolis** to confirm. **Keep travelling** closes the warning without founding. In exchange:

- The Host's people become your city's population.
- Everything carried — grain and silver — pours into the city. A new city starts with **no timber and no stone**: its [[catchment]] needs ground that produces these materials (or a neighbour to buy from) before the first building can rise.
- Forces standing on the hex become its garrison.
- **Demeter's gift**: found inland, with farmable ground in your catchment, and a Farm appears, free.
- **Poseidon's gift**: found on the coast and a galley is waiting in the harbour — instead of the farm. The sea feeds a harbour city; a gift is one or the other, never both.
- Your first [[citizens]] are placed on the best food land automatically, until the city feeds itself — and always at least one, so even a colony of 100 has someone in the fields. The rest stand idle, waiting for you.

The forecast lists grain and fish production separately from the population's full food consumption. A production deficit uses up carried grain before hunger begins. Fish counts as food. Hunger begins only when daily meals are not fully covered, after stored grain, fish, oil, wine and emergency livestock have been used. A production deficit warns of future hunger once reserves run out; it does not by itself mean the population is starving.

If the known catchment has no timber or cedar to cover timber costs, the forecast warns before you found. Unknown ground may still hold wood; scout it or plan deliveries. A fully known catchment without wood needs imports for building.

Place citizens on olive groves, maquis, hills or river valleys for timber, or on cedar forest for cedar to cover timber costs, and on stone-bearing ground for stone; idle citizens produce nothing. Maquis, hills and river valleys each have one timber place before a Lumbermill is built, yielding 0.33 timber per worker per game day ([[catchment]]). The forecast says when Demeter’s farm is already included; another farm is not an extra founding gift.

On grain-bearing hills, a Farm at level 1 makes 0.85 grain per worker per game day, with room for five workers. Plain hills give 0.5 per worker. With another grain-bearing hill in reach, six food workers can feed a new city of 1,000 people; leave the rest for timber and stone. Check the forecast for your actual site.

Every hex of the world has one kind of ground. The ground decides what your [[citizens|citizens]] can work there, which buildings can stand on it, and how long it takes to cross: on foot, by cart or by ship. A city works the hexes of its [[catchment]], so the ground around a site decides what the city can make.

**Coast is not a kind of ground.** A hex is coastal when it borders the sea; a city on such a hex may build a [[harbour|Harbour]] and a [[shipyard|Shipyard]].

## All terrain

<!-- list: terrain -->
| Name | In short |
|---|---|
| [[plains]] | Plains are open land suitable for grain fields. |
| [[olive-grove]] | An olive grove is wooded land that supports timber and the city’s olive economy. |
| [[hills]] | Hills are land with grain, stone and possible mineral deposits. |
| [[limestone-mountains]] | Limestone mountains are difficult ground with strong stone-quarrying potential. |
| [[red-mountains]] | Red mountains are difficult ground that may contain strategic ore deposits. |
| [[coastal-sea]] | Coastal sea is navigable water beside land, with fishing places. |
| [[lake]] | A lake is still water inland, cut off from the Thalassa; its fish feed the cities on its shore. |
| [[deep-sea]] | Deep sea is open navigable water; fishing there requires a Harbour. |
| [[rivers]] | A river carries ships: downstream in half the time, upstream in twice. The first ruler whose people reach one may name it. |
| [[river-valley]] | A river valley is land beside a river, suitable for settlement and production. |
| [[river-delta]] | A river delta is productive land near a river’s mouth. |
| [[river-ford]] | A ford is shallow river ground where land travel can cross the water. |
| [[maquis]] | Maquis is Mediterranean scrub that can provide timber. |
| [[semi-desert]] | Semi-desert is dry ground with limited food and costly land travel. |
| [[cedar-forest]] | A cedar forest is wooded ground where citizens can gather cedar. |
<!-- /list -->

How long each kind of ground takes to cross is in [[marching]]. What lies under the ground ([[copper|copper]], [[tin|tin]], [[silver|silver]], [[incense|incense]]) is marked on the hex itself; see [[goods]] and [[bronze]].

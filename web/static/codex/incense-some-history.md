Bronze Age temples burned resin, not one perfume. Around the east of the Mediterranean the common kind was the sap of the terebinth tree (*Pistacia*), gathered on the dry hills of Kna'an and Cyprus and shipped in jars. The wreck at Uluburun, sunk off the south coast of Anatolia at the end of the thirteenth century before our era, carried about a hundred and fifty such jars among its copper and tin. Amphorae from Kna'an turn up in Egypt with traces of the same resin. The rarer frankincense and myrrh came to Egypt from Punt, far down the Red Sea, in small and precious loads; labdanum, scraped from the rock-rose shrubs of Crete, was the Minoan smell. Here they are all one good, **Incense**.

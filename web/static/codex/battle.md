**There is no declaration of war.** If your army arrives where a hostile army is, or a unit on watch catches a march going by, a battle starts by itself.

## How a battle runs

A battle is not one roll. It runs over several days, and it always resolves the same way from the moment it begins. What decides it:

- **How many of your men actually fight.** Only part of each side engages each round — set mostly by [[loyalty]], nudged up by the gods' favour ([[kharis]]) and by the **fortify** stance ([[stances]]). Loyal troops commit; wavering ones hold back.
- **What kind of men they are.** An ordinary soldier strikes once; elite infantry three times as hard; war chariots five. This is what [[bronze]] buys.
- **Walls**, if a city is involved. A wall absorbs a fixed amount of damage each day — a shield, not a sword. **storm** halves it, at a price.
- **When they break.** A side that has lost enough routs and withdraws with its survivors. Annihilation is the exception. You choose how much is "enough" for your whole realm under **War → Army → When to retreat** — a share of losses, **hold to the last man**, or by the troops' loyalty (the default) — and can override it for one unit in the battle it is fighting; see [[stances]].

## After the battle

You are told the outcome, with the losses on both sides — for example *"Victory at Petras — your spearmen (100→61) vs Idomeneus's chariots (40→12). The field was taken."* You hear the clash of arms if sound is on.

Winning at a city does not make it yours. It becomes **occupied** — see [[occupation]]. A city can also be taken without any battle at all, by hunger: see [[sieges]].

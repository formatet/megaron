Your people eat every day, in a fixed order: **grain first, then fish, then livestock.** Fish covers only what grain could not; livestock is slaughtered only when both are gone. A coastal city with good fishing is fed differently from an inland one.

## When there is not enough

If all three run out and the day's need is still unmet:

- the settlement **loses part of its population that day**, and keeps losing while the shortfall lasts;
- below about a hundred people, **the settlement collapses and is gone.**

You get an urgent notification the first day it happens, and starving cities always sit at the top of the archive. Starvation is slow enough to fix and fast enough to kill — act in the same session.

## What to do about it

- Put more [[citizens]] on grain or fish hexes. Check the *next citizen would add* figure.
- Build or level a **Farm** ([[buildings]]) — it raises how many people a grain hex can hold.
- Bring food in from another of your cities by [[transfers|transfer]] or a [[routes|standing order]].
- Buy it ([[trade]]).
- Shrink what eats: soldiers are drafted from your people and eat like them ([[upkeep]]).

The [[sitos|Sitos granary]] is your buffer against a bad stretch. A city under [[sieges|siege]] starves because its fields are cut off, not because it produces too little.

Growth is covered in [[growth]].

A city grows when it has a **surplus of food** — when it produces more food each day than its people eat. Growing costs no food: the surplus stays in the same city grain, fish, oil and wine stocks that feed your people.

- **Surplus, not store.** What counts is the daily balance — grain, fish, oil and wine produced, minus what the people eat — shown as the food *net* on the city. A positive net grows the city; zero or below holds it steady, even with full stores.
- **Surplus size.** A bigger surplus fills your stores faster; it does not itself raise the growth rate.
- **Variety.** Any one of the four food goods feeds people. Growth gains 10% per extra kind actually eaten that day, up to the existing variety ceiling: two kinds give a 1.1 multiplier and four give 1.3. Livestock counts only if eaten as emergency food. A good merely sitting in store gives no variety bonus.
- **Size.** Growth slows as a city approaches its practical ceiling.

In short: a city grows only while it makes more food than it eats; it holds steady when it does not; and it shrinks when it goes hungry. The city screen and `keryx status` say which of the three it is, and the food net behind it, for example "not growing: food net -1.7 per day (needs more than 0)".

More people eat more, so a growing city eats into its own surplus until the balance reaches zero and growth stops — unless you put the new citizens to work on food.

Each time the population crosses another hundred, a new [[citizens|citizen]] appears and places itself on your best food hex.

More people means more workers, more recruits and more mouths. See [[food]].

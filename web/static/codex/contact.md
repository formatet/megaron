You cannot write to, or trade with, a city you have never reached. A settlement becomes reachable — **permanently** — once one of these has happened:

- you own it;
- **one of your messengers has physically arrived there**, ever — even if it has long since come home;
- one of your marches has passed over it.

Contact is earned by walking, and once earned it never lapses. Sending a messenger to a stranger's city is not only a letter — it is the act that opens [[trade]] with them for good.

## Permanent contact with a city {#contact-details}

**Rumour is not contact.** [[gossip|Gossip]] tells you that a city exists and roughly where, deliberately blurred, but it does not open the door. Seeing a city on the map ([[sight]]) does not either.

Cities you have contacted appear in **Diplomacy → Letters → Write**. Choose **Write** to ask their Wanax what they need or can spare; market knowledge comes through [[messengers|correspondence]]. A city's [[market|market board]] tells you whom to ask.

Contact reveals that city’s location, not the surrounding land or its current activity. What your people saw inside it is described in [[foreign-cities]].

**A letter you receive tells you who wrote, not where they live.** It opens no contact and puts nothing on your map. You can still answer it — your reply goes home with the same [[runners|runner]] while it waits.

Opening a letter in an exchange marks it as read. The Diplomacy badge counts unread letters; an unanswered offer can remain pending after you have read it. `keryx inbox` marks the letters it displays as read too. Reading does not send the messenger home or accept a trade.

### Keeping and reading received letters {#contact-details-2}

Received letters remain in Letters after the runner leaves or an offer closes. Use `keryx inbox --history` to read past correspondence; plain `keryx inbox` lists current messages and pending offers.

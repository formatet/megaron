Soldiers eat **food: grain, fish, oil and wine**, shared in proportion to the stocks available. Professionals also need **silver pay** every game day.

## Pay — silver

**Field pay starts at 1 silver per 100 people per game day**, the wage of a worker. Spearmen draw **0 silver**. A full hundred-man elite infantry cohort draws **1.5 silver in the field and 0.75 at home**; a full chariot cohort draws **4 in the field and 2 at home**. Remaining men determine the bill, so a half-strength cohort draws half the pay.

**Home pay is always half field pay.** Garrison, repairs and fitting out count as home. Marching, posted troops, freight missions and soldiers embarked aboard a ship count as field service. Soldiers’ pay does not flow back into the city: the stated silver bill is what the city loses.

**Only War Galley rowers draw silver pay**, per surviving crew member at 1 silver per hundred in the field and half at home. A full War Galley (50 rowers) draws **0.5 silver in the field / 0.25 at home**. Galleys and Emporoi draw **0 silver**, in port and at sea, and cannot desert for lack of silver. Carried soldiers draw their own field pay in addition to their ship’s crew.

If you cannot pay, you are warned first; if it goes on, **men desert**, and a unit that loses everyone is gone. Recruit professionals only when you can keep paying them. A spearman cohort costs 12 grain and 100 people to recruit, with no silver.

An empty ship **Laid up** in its own Harbour costs **0 silver and 0 ship food**. Its crew has returned to the city’s population and eats as civilians. **Fit out** drafts that crew back immediately; home-rate ship food and, for a War Galley, half field pay resume while the ship spends three game days preparing. See [[sea]].

## Food

Soldiers use the same four [[food|food goods]] as the people. Livestock remains emergency food for civilians, not an army ration. A full spearman cohort uses **0.5 food per game day in garrison**, or **1 food in the field**. A unit in garrison eats from its city. **A unit in the field eats double**, and there is nothing to forage — so a long march, a siege line or a posted watch is a real cost ([[stances]]). <!-- src: server/internal/combat/upkeep.go upkeepFieldGrainFactor -->

**Rowers eat like soldiers.** A ship feeds its actual surviving crew: each rower uses the same food as a spearman. A full Galley (20 rowers) uses **0.10 food per game day in port** or **0.20 on a mission**; a War Galley (50) uses **0.25 / 0.50**, and an Emporos (10) **0.05 / 0.10**. Fewer surviving rowers eat less. Garrisoned ships, repairs and fitting out use the port ration. Marching, posted and freighting ships use the field ration, including a freight route's wait between legs. A laid-up hull has no ship ration because its crew has returned to the city. Ship pay follows the home and field rates above.

A unit that runs out of food **starves**: it loses men each game day, and can starve to nothing. A ship turns home when its remaining food is only enough for the way back, unless it can reach a city for provisions. Its owner hears the reason when it reaches home, not from far away; see [[ship-food-return]].

The Notifications archive marks when a city’s garrison starts losing men to hunger and when it stops. A recovery means that loss has stopped; it does not promise food for every soldier who may arrive later. Produce more food or send food by [[transfers|transfer]].

## Before you settle

Your [[horde|Nomadic Host]]'s spearman escort draws no silver. The silver reserve you carry goes into the city when you settle; it does not limit how long the escort can travel.

## The rule of thumb

Your army is only as large as your economy can feed and pay. Build [[food]] surplus before you build cohorts, and [[silver]] income before expanding your professional forces ([[units]]).

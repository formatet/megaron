**A trade offer travels attached to a letter.** There is no market screen and no public listings. You write to a specific city and pin an offer to the message — so you can only trade with cities you have [[contact|contacted]].

## Making an offer

In **Diplomacy → Compose** (or inside a conversation), expand **+ Attach trade offer** and choose:

- **Buy** — the good you want, the quantity, and the silver you offer. Your silver is held in escrow.
- **Sell** — the good you offer, the quantity, and the silver you want. Your goods are held in escrow.

Then **Dispatch →**. The offer walks with the [[messengers|messenger]], so it takes game-days to arrive.

## Answering an offer

An incoming offer appears in the conversation, for example *"Wants 40 tin · Offers 200 silver"*, with **Accept ✓** and **Decline ✗**. Your own pending offers carry **Cancel offer ✗** — withdraw it and your escrow comes back. If nobody answers before it expires, the escrow comes back too. Nothing is ever lost to silence.

You are notified when an offer is accepted, declined or expires.

## Delivery

An accepted trade is carried by caravan. It arrives as a *Trade delivered* notification. A negotiated delivery can be lost to storms or pirates on the way, and like any [[transfers|cargo]] it can be raided.

### When it goes by sea

If both cities are coastal or harboured and a sea lane connects them, the trade sails instead of walking — carried by **your own** ship, whichever of you *sent* the offer. It makes the whole round trip: out with your half of the deal when the other side accepts, home again with their half. The other side's ships are never touched.

If you send an offer and have no free Galley or Emporos in your own port, and no land road connects the two cities either, the offer cannot be sent at all. If your only ship is taken by something else between sending and the other side's answer, their **Accept** fails and tells them so — your offer simply stays open until it expires or a ship frees up. A shipment too heavy for the ship's hold is refused the same way, naming the capacity.

The ship is not free for anything else until it is home — outbound and return both count, same [[transfers|three fates]] (captured, damaged, sunk) as any other cargo at sea. A **Damaged** outcome on the way home still gets half the cargo back to you, never to the other side.

## Finding partners

**Economy → Wants** lists shortages and surpluses in every city you have contacted. An empty list means either that everyone you have reached is balanced, or that you have reached nobody. Both point to the same move: make contact somewhere new.

What is most worth trading? [[silver]], and the half of [[bronze]] you do not have.

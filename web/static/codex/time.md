## Game days and the clock

The world advances one **game day** at a time. Wherever the game tells you how long something takes — a march, a building, a letter — it counts in game days, because that is the unit your decisions are made in.

How much real time a game day takes is set per world. A test world can run faster than normal. The **date in the top bar** is the world's own calendar; the sun and moon beside it show the time in the world. **When something happens** uses your clock and date: **today 18:34**, **tomorrow 09:10**, or **Thu 09:00**; further away, a date is shown. **How long something lasts** stays in game days of the world: **food for 12 game days**, or **a 30-game-day expedition**. Arrival estimates follow the world's current pace; they can change when the world pauses or its pace changes.

## The world does not wait for you

Everything keeps happening while you are away: production, eating, [[upkeep|pay]], marches, battles, the gods' moods. That is why the most useful skills in Megaron are the ones that work while you are absent — [[routes|standing orders]], well-filled [[food|food stores]], a [[stances|watch]] on the approaches — and why the [[coming-back|Notifications archive]] never throws anything away.

## Before the world begins

A new world's clock is frozen until enough Wanaxes have joined (see [[getting-in]]). You can look around while you wait, but no orders: building, recruiting, placing people, marching, founding your city, sending messengers — each is refused with *"The world has not begun"* and a count of how many Wanaxes have arrived. Give them once the world starts.

Beside a planned duration, details show about how many real hours it takes at this world’s current pace and the estimated local completion time. Dense rate tables use **per game day**. The same number of game days may take a different amount of real time in another world or after its pace changes; the live estimate uses this world’s clock.

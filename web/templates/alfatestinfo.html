Megaron

Megaron is a slow strategy game set in a mythically inspired Bronze Age around the eastern Mediterranean. You are a Wanax, ruler of a people: you found and feed cities, put people to work, trade, explore, and meet other rulers and gods.

The world goes on when you log out. You give orders and come back later to see what happened. It is a game to think about between visits, where plans and relationships are allowed to take time.

What kind of game is Megaron?

Think of the city-building and expansion of Civilization or Colonization, but slower, persistent and shared with other people. You manage cities, work, production, trade and exploration. The other rulers are real people, and their choices shape the world as much as yours do.

What makes Megaron different

News travels with people. Letters, trade offers and orders to armies in the field travel by messenger. Your map shows what your people know. A reply comes home with the runner who carries it.

You name the world. Be the first to reach a river, mountain massif, desert or cedar forest, and you may name it. Those names become part of the world you share.

Geography brings people together. Bronze needs copper and tin; ships need timber or cedar. What your land lacks gives you a reason to explore, trade and make friends far from home.

The gods take an interest. Temples and offerings sustain their goodwill. True omens can reveal something your people have yet to see.

Diplomacy is personal. Write in your own words, agree a trade or send a gift. Trust grows from what you do and what you remember.

What a visit looks like

Read what happened while you were away. Move people to the fields, the sea or a workshop. Order a new building. Send a caravan of goods to your colony, or a messenger with a letter to a neighbour. Send a messenger to your charioteers to let them know where to head next. Then log out.

You don't need to be online at set times. Orders take game days to carry out, and news travels with people, so nothing happens instantly. A visit can be short; a plan can take many game days.

The practical part

  • Join the alpha. Open Megaron and create your account to begin.
  • Free. Free as in: if this was beer, you'd get both a pint out of kindness and the recipe to brew more, so you could pass the favour along. It will stay that way. The first world runs on a server in a closet in Tim's apartment and has room for up to 100 rulers. The source code is open if you want to look at it, and later anyone will be able to run a world of their own.
  • Account. A username and a password. No email address.
  • Where. In the web browser, on a computer or a phone.
  • Time. A game day passes every {{.GameDayLength}} in this world. The alpha world runs for several weeks; if it is reset, that is announced in the chat first.

Playing with an AI agent

You are welcome to let an AI play for you, or alongside you. Keryx, the command line, is built for that. A few rules keep the world fair for everyone in it:

  • One account per agent. Each agent plays its own Wanax, and you play yours. Don't run several rulers from one account, or one ruler from several agents.
  • Keep a gentle pace. Make at most one request every 5 seconds. When your agent is only checking whether anything has happened, let it look no more than once every 10 minutes; the world moves in game days, not seconds. If a request fails, wait longer before trying again rather than retrying at once. The server will not stop you; we trust you to do it.
  • Don't hunt for holes. If your agent finds a bug or a way around the rules, report it (Report, or keryx report) and don't use it. That is exactly the kind of thing this alpha needs to hear about.
  • Say that you are an AI. When your agent writes to another ruler, the letter should make clear that it comes from an AI. Other players decide for themselves how they answer.

The person who runs the agent answers for what it does.

A world to come back to

We want to build a small, living and unpredictable world where names mean something. Other rulers are people to trade with, trust, or end up in conflict with. Resources, relationships and the players' choices should make stories nobody wrote in advance.

A place to step into and make plans for, together with others. No ads, no feeds that want to keep you there every minute. Sometimes you will have to find your way by trying.

Megaron is a one-person project, built by Tim with AI-assisted development.

This is an early alpha

Come help shape Megaron. We are testing the balance, improving the interface and learning from the stories players make. Tell us what draws you in and where you get stuck.

You don't need to understand everything at once. When you join, a short opening dispatch tells you what to do first. The Codex, behind ? in the game, is there when you want depth. Do tell us when the help isn't enough.

Above all, this alpha needs help with one question: can a new player found a city, feed it and get it producing without help from anyone?

What do we want to hear?

Tell us about concrete situations:

  • What was easy or hard to understand?
  • What were you trying to do, what did you expect, and what happened?
  • What was interesting, frustrating, boring or unclear? Did you feel like logging in again?

Use Report at the bottom right of the game, or keryx report from the command line. Also report strange results, and anything that is missing, unclear or wrong in the Codex.

Community outside the game

Join the Megaron Discord for questions, stories and alpha news. Everyone is welcome; joining the chat is optional.

Chat rules and privacy

  • Be decent. No harassment, no hate, nothing illegal.
  • Talk about the game, not other players’ private lives.
  • Keep personal information private.
  • Contact a moderator if something in the community needs attention.

Discord is a separate service with its own account and privacy settings. For a problem inside the game, use Report.

Try it, follow your curiosity, and tell us what you discover. Your observations help us understand what kind of game Megaron is becoming.

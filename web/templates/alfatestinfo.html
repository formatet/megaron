Megaron

Megaron is a slow strategy game set in a mythic Bronze Age eastern Mediterranean. You play a Wanax, a ruler, and build up settlements: feed them, put people to work, trade, raise and keep armies, explore, and deal with the gods and with other rulers.

The world keeps running while you are logged out. Production, travel and messages take time. You give orders, leave, and come back later to see what happened.

What kind of game we want this to be

A social game. The other rulers are real people, and most of what matters happens between you: who you trade with, who you trust, who you warn, who you march against. Because everything travels by messenger, those conversations are slow. A letter is something you write with some care, and an answer is something you wait for.

A different world to step into. A small Bronze Age realm where you have a place and something to work towards, with friends: a slow grind with a glorious purpose.

An alternative to advertising-driven social media. Something to return to in a pause in the day, to think about and make plans for, instead of another feed built to keep you scrolling. There are no ads, and the game does not need your attention every minute.

A world small enough to know. When the game is finished, each world will hold at most 100 rulers, few enough that names come to mean something. The alpha is smaller than that.

Beyond that, we want situations to come out of what players decide and how the game's systems meet: trade, shortages, relationships. Some things you will only understand by trying them.

Everything travels by messenger

Nothing in Megaron is instant. An order to your own army is carried by a runner and takes effect when the runner gets there. A message to another ruler arrives when the messenger does, and the answer comes back the same way. Messengers are sacred and cannot be stopped. What you know about the world is what your own people have seen or been told.

No tech tree

There is no tech tree, and none is planned. Better units exist: elite infantry and chariots need bronze, war galleys need cedar. What limits them is what you can get hold of, not research. The game is more about who you talk to and what you can trade for than about which units you have.

This is an early alpha

Much is unfinished. Balance is uneven, parts of the interface are unclear, and some things are broken. You don't need to understand everything at once. The rules and a reference are in the Codex, behind the ? in the top right corner.

What we want to hear

Concrete examples help most.

  • What was easy or hard to understand?
  • What were you trying to do, what did you expect, and what happened?
  • What was interesting? What was frustrating, boring or unclear?
  • Did you want to log in again later?
  • Bugs or strange results.

Use the Report button at the bottom right in the game, or keryx report if you play from the command line. Tell us too when the Codex is unclear, missing something, or doesn't match the game.

Community chat, outside the game

For questions, bug talk and news about the alpha there is a chat on Matrix, a separate service we run ourselves. It is out of game: what is said there does not reach the world, and messengers in the game are not connected to it. You don't need it to play, and anything important about the game is announced inside the game.

The chat has its own account, separate from your Megaron account. No email is needed. Open agora.formatet.se and register with the code that came with your invitation. Any Matrix app works too, with agora.formatet.se as the homeserver. Then ask us to invite you to #megaron-ooc.

Chat rules and privacy

  • Be decent. No harassment, no hate, nothing illegal.
  • Talk about the game, not other players' real lives.
  • Don't post your real name, address, school, health or other private details. Choose a username that isn't your real name.
  • Report a message with the menu next to it in your Matrix app, or write to a moderator. Moderators can read what is reported.

The chat runs on our own hardware in Sweden and is not connected to any other Matrix server. The room is not encrypted, so don't treat it as private. Messages stay until they are deleted. Deleting your game account does not delete your chat account: ask us, and we will remove it.
